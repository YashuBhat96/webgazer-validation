Webcam eye-tracking · setup

Gaze Check

This uses your webcam to work out where you're looking on the screen. It takes about five minutes: a couple of quick measurements, a short calibration, and two tasks. Nothing is recorded except where your eyes point — no video ever leaves your computer. Work in a well-lit room, and once you start, try to keep your head fairly still.

Step 1 — Tell us your screen size

Find any card the size of a bank card or ID card (they're all the same size). We'll use it as a real-world ruler so the measurements come out in the right units.

  1. Hold the card flat against your screen — literally touching the glass, over the grey bar below.
  2. Drag the slider until the grey bar is exactly as wide as your card.
cm — leave as is for a normal card

You'll sit wherever is comfortable — the camera measures your distance in the next step and uses it, so accuracy still comes out in degrees.

Step 2 — Set yourself up well

Eye tracking from a webcam is only as good as your setup. Five quick things make a big difference:

  1. Raise your webcam to eye level, pointing straight at your face — prop your laptop up on some books if you need to. This is the single biggest fix for accuracy, especially up and down.
  2. Light on your face, not behind you. Face a window or lamp; don't sit with a bright window behind you.
  3. Take glasses off if you comfortably can — reflections throw the tracker off.
  4. Keep your head still once you start, and stay at roughly one distance.
  5. Close other heavy apps and tabs — the tracker needs the processing power.

Step 3 — Turn on your camera

When you press the button your browser will ask to use the camera — choose Allow. A small picture of yourself appears; move so your face sits inside the box.

Step 3 — get comfortable

Find your spot

Your camera is measuring how far you are from the screen (it reads the size of your iris — you don't have to do anything). The big number below is your current distance. Sit however is comfortable, then hold still — once the number settles it turns green and Continue switches on, and the tool simply uses whatever distance you're at. Just try to stay about there for the rest of the session.

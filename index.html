Webcam eye tracking

Gaze Check

This uses your webcam to work out where you're looking on the screen. It takes about five minutes: a short setup, a camera check, a 30-second calibration and a 30-second accuracy check. Only the estimated gaze position and your answers to the questions are recorded. No video leaves your computer.

You’ll need: a bank or ID card (to measure your screen) and a tape measure or ruler (to measure how far you sit from the camera). At the end, you’ll download your data files and email them to the researcher.

Use the ID you were given. It only labels your data files.

Vision questions

Set up · 4 of 5

Measure your screen

Any bank card or ID card works as a ruler (they're all the same size). This lets us report accuracy in real-world units.

  1. Place the card flat against your screen, touching the glass.
  2. Drag the slider (or use − and +) until the grey bar is exactly the same width as your card.

We kept the width from your last session on this computer. Check it still matches your card.

Set up · 5 of 5

Set yourself up well

Webcam eye tracking is only as good as the setup. These five things make the biggest difference:

  1. Raise your webcam to eye level, pointing straight at your face. Prop your laptop on some books if you need to.
  2. Light on your face, not behind you. Face a window or lamp; avoid a bright window behind you.
  3. Glasses off if you can see the screen clearly without them; reflections on the lenses confuse the tracker. If you can’t, keep them on. Contact lenses are fine, so keep them in.
  4. Sit 50–70 cm from the screen, about an arm’s length, and stay in that position until everything is finished. Keep your head still.
  5. Close other heavy apps and tabs. The tracker needs the processing power.
